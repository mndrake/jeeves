# M2 - Live transcript

Status: code complete (2026-11-02); Mac tests pending. PRD: `docs/prd/02-transcription.md`.

M0 settled transcription latency differently than planned: Apple's SpeechAnalyzer
finalizes about 1.1 s after the speaker stops, so Jeeves acts on the latest partial at
400 ms of silence instead. M2 answers the two questions that left open:

1. **Accuracy on Jeeves's vocabulary.** SpeechAnalyzer heard "Claude Code" as "cloud code"
   every time in M0. Is NVIDIA's Parakeet model (via FluidAudio), with or without
   vocabulary boosting, better? And how much does the alias table recover?
2. **Is the 400 ms partial good enough?** How often does the text Jeeves acts on differ
   from the final, and can a fast Parakeet second pass on each utterance give better text
   in time?

First use downloads Parakeet (about 0.5 GB) and, for boosting, a CTC model (about
100 MB) into FluidAudio's cache. Later runs load from disk.

## Tests

From the unzipped `jeeves-spikes` folder (v0.2.0 or later).

### 1. Synthetic speech baseline (no call needed)

```bash
cd ~/jeeves-spikes
./asr/make-tts-eval.sh asr-eval              # system voice; add voices: ... Samantha Daniel
./spike-asr asr-eval/*.wav --terms asr/terms.txt --out asr-tts.json
```

Compares `apple`, `parakeet` and `parakeet-boost`, each raw and with aliases. Synthetic
speech is a best case; this checks the tool and gives a first ranking.

### 2. Your voice through a call (the real test)

Join a Meet call from your phone and read `asr/read-aloud.txt` aloud into the phone at a
normal pace while the Mac records the call:

```bash
./spike-capture --minutes 3 --report-every 30 --wav asr-eval/me-call.wav
cp asr/read-aloud.txt asr-eval/me-call.txt
./spike-asr asr-eval/me-call.wav --terms asr/terms.txt --engines apple,apple-accurate,parakeet,parakeet-boost --out asr-me.json
```

Optionally repeat with `--model ultra` for Parakeet.

### 3. Live: partial vs final, and the second pass

With a call or a YouTube talk playing:

```bash
./spike-stt --live --seconds 120
./spike-stt --live --seconds 120 --second-pass parakeet-boost
```

Each detected end of speech prints `>> at 400 ms of silence: ...` (what Jeeves would act
on); a final that differs prints `<< final differs ...`; the second pass prints its text
and how long after the speaker stopped it was ready. The summary reports the share of
identical provisional texts and the second-pass latency.

## Decision rules

- **Engine for search and answers:** whichever of `apple+aliases`, `parakeet+aliases`,
  `parakeet-boost+aliases` has the best term recall on test 2, unless its WER is more than
  2 points worse than the best.
- **Second pass:** adopt if its text is ready within about 300 ms of the 400 ms trigger
  (so under about 700 ms after speech ends, p95) and it beats the provisional text on
  terms. Otherwise act on the provisional text and correct with the final.
- **Partial is good enough** if at least 80% of provisional texts are identical to the
  final after aliases, or differ only in words that do not change search results.

## Exit criteria (from PLAN.md, updated by M0)

- [ ] Engine (or engine plus second pass) chosen by test 2's term recall and WER.
- [ ] Provisional text quality measured live (share identical to final).
- [ ] Domain-term recall at least 90% on test 2 after aliases or boosting.
